# v0.43.0 — Search the shelf, and invent a step

## Problem Statement

Two unrelated walls, one release.

### 1. ADD GEAR is a scroll, not a shelf

The catalog browser's character-card tabs are a carousel you swipe. Its four ITEM tabs — Weapons,
Armor, Loot, Consumables — are a flat list, and each one is enormous: 60 loot items, 60 consumables,
and every weapon and every armor of a tier, plus whatever homebrew a player has installed. There is
no way to ask for a thing by name. Finding the Broadsword means scrolling past everything that is not
the Broadsword.

Three specific failures:

- **No search anywhere in the item tabs.** The tier chips narrow weapons and armor to a quarter of
  the list and nothing narrows loot or consumables at all.
- **A weapon's row does not say how many hands it takes.** `burden` is on every `WeaponDef` and on
  every homebrew `WeaponSpec`, and the row prints trait, range and damage and then stops. One-handed
  or two-handed decides whether you can carry a shield, which is the single most consequential fact
  about a weapon after its damage, and the browser hides it.
- **The DM's own workflow is unsupported.** Loot and consumables are indexed by a d100-style ROLL in
  the rulebook: the DM rolls, reads "Table 1, 34", and then has to find that item by eye in a list of
  sixty. The number they already hold is the fastest possible query and there is nowhere to type it.

### 2. An expansion can add content, but it cannot add a STEP

An author can make a class, a domain, an ancestry, a community, gear. Every one of those slots into a
step character creation ALREADY has. There is no way to invent a new one.

The owner's case: a campaign in the world of Brandon Sanderson's Windrunners, where a character
belongs to one of the Orders of the Knights Radiant. That is not an ancestry, not a community, not a
subclass. It is a new question the character creator should ask, with a set of cards as its answers,
and the app has no shape for it.

Underneath that, three problems with how the authoring surface treats the things that ARE systems:

- **A class card and a domain card look like an ordinary card while you are making them.** They have
  a body text area, section rows, an effects list. But a class card is not a card somebody plays with
  a body you write once; it is the START of a system that will have subclasses and features hanging
  off it. Nothing in the UI says so, and the sections you are offered are the wrong priority.
- **The type chip cannot be customised on the very cards whose identity matters most.** A class card
  always says CLASS in the same red-on-darker-red. A domain always says DOMAIN. The owner: "I can't
  customize the chip. It will always say class with a red and darker red gradient, and I don't like
  that."
- **Class styling is not inherited by a page, despite v0.42.7 shipping exactly that.** Making a class,
  then a second page of it, shows a preview in the default paint. The identity resolver is correct;
  the preview card handed to it is missing the one field it tests.

## Solution

### A. The catalog browser learns to be asked

**A search field, under the narrowing controls.** For Weapons and Armor it sits below the Tier row;
for Loot and Consumables it sits below the Homebrew/Official chips when those exist, and directly
below the tabs when they do not. One field, all four tabs, cleared on tab change.

Matching is by TOKEN over everything the row knows: name, trait, range, damage, damage type, burden,
slot, tier, the feature name and text, the roll number, the table. Every whitespace-separated token
must match somewhere, so "two hand" finds two-handed weapons, "d10 agility" finds agile heavy hitters
and "34" finds roll 34. Case- and punctuation-insensitive: "twohanded", "two-handed" and "two handed"
are one query.

**Burden on every weapon row**, official and homebrew, in the same subtitle the rest of the stats
live in.

**A roll pad for Loot and Consumables.** A `#` button beside the search field opens a number pad over
the browser — over, not under: it publishes through the panel's existing `OverlayHost`, so it is
drawn at the panel root and cannot end up behind it. It carries Table 1 / Table 2 chips (Table 2 only
when Hope and Fear is enabled for this character) and accepts 1 to 60. OK resolves that roll to its
item and adds it, which is the DM's whole loop in three taps.

### B. A new KIND of thing an expansion can make

**The Type.** A fourth system card, beside the class, the domain and the subclass. Making one does
not make a card somebody holds; it declares a category of card that then exists everywhere cards
exist.

A type carries:

- **A name** — "Order of the Knights Radiant".
- **A chip** — the plaque its cards wear: the word, a two-stop gradient, and the text colour, with a
  live preview of the chip itself.
- **A creation step, optionally** — when on, character creation asks this question. Where: right
  beside Transformations, which is where the owner wants every custom step, whether or not
  transformations themselves are enabled. How many cards it asks for is a number the author sets, so
  "pick one Order" and "pick three Blessings" are the same feature.

Cards OF a type are authored in the same expansion and simply name it. They are offered in the
library's type chooser as first-class options ("+ Order of the Knights Radiant"), so an author who
has made the type finds it exactly where they make everything else.

**Where a type's cards show up**, all of it falling out of one field:

- **Character creation** — as its own step, beside Transformations, if the author turned it on.
- **The card archive** — its own filter chip, alongside Domains, Ancestry and the rest.
- **The pack's own gallery** — as cards like any other, wearing the chip they inherit.
- **ADD GEAR** — its own tab, named after the type, so a player can be handed one mid-campaign.
- **The sheet's card type picker** — a player with the pack can write a card of that type.

### C. The system cards stop pretending to be cards

**Class, Domain and Type are TEMPLATES, and their editors say so.** The body area is replaced by a
plain statement of what is being made and what will hang off it, in the place the body used to be.
No section rows, no effects list: neither means anything on a card that is a declaration. What is
left is the identity — the name, the art, the colour and the chip — which is exactly what a template
should own.

**The chip is editable on all three.** Its word, its two colours and its text colour, previewed as
the chip, immediately, on the card above.

**And it is INHERITED**, exactly as the class's colour and banner already are: a card belonging to a
class wears the class's chip unless it has one of its own; a domain card wears its domain's; a card
of a type wears the type's. Resolved when the pack is present, and stamped onto the copy embedded on
a character so it survives the pack being deleted.

**The page preview bug is fixed** at its root: the preview card was built without `classSpec`, so
`inheritsClassLook` could not tell a page from a class and refused to dress it. A second bug fell out
of the same trace: the save dropped `className` for every card of type `class`, pages included, so a
saved page had nothing pointing at its class at all.

**A base class card prints its summary.** The class form asks for the class in two or three sentences
and that answer went nowhere; a template's body is what it declares, so that is what it prints now.

**Import stops downgrading content.** `normalizeLibraryCard` did not list `customDomain` or `feature`
among the types it accepts, so importing a pack quietly turned both into plain Cards. `type` would
have joined them.

## Non-Goals

- A gradient EDITOR (angle, midpoints, more than two stops). Two stops and a text colour is what a
  10dp band can carry.
- Custom steps in the CHARACTERIZE flow. A stat block being turned into a character is not choosing
  an Order.
- A type's cards getting a deck of their own on the sheet. They land in the arsenal, which is where
  every other acquired card of consequence lands.

## Acceptance

1. Typing "two" in Weapons leaves only two-handed weapons; typing "34" in Loot leaves roll 34.
2. Every weapon row, official or homebrew, prints One-Handed or Two-Handed.
3. `#` in Loot opens a pad ABOVE the browser, Table 1/2 + 1–60, and OK adds that exact item.
4. A type made in an expansion appears in the type chooser, the archive, the pack gallery, ADD GEAR
   and (when enabled) character creation.
5. A creation step made from a type asks for the author's number of cards and sits beside Transform.
6. A class card, a domain and a type show no body/sections/effects and do show a chip editor.
7. A second page of a homebrew class opens already wearing the class's colour, banner and chip.
