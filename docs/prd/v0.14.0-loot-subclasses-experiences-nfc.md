# v0.14.0 — Loot, subclass families, experience modifiers, and NFC that keeps its decoration

## Problem Statement

Eight problems, from the player's chair:

1. **Sharing a card over NFC feels broken and loses the card.** The receive confirmation shows a
   rendered card that bleeds three-quarters of the way out of its own panel. The arrival animation
   pops onto the screen abruptly instead of arriving. There is no way to tell the phone even noticed
   a tag until the panel appears. Worst of all, a weapon shared out of the Card Library arrives on the
   other phone as a flat rectangle of color — no sword glyph, no stat block, no weapon plaque, and any
   uploaded art is gone entirely.
2. **Level up sometimes hands over the wrong domain card.** The player centers a card, presses
   "Choose this card", and a *different* card — one or two positions away — is what ends up on the
   sheet. It is intermittent and cannot be reproduced on demand, which makes it feel haunted.
3. **A custom subclass card doesn't say what it is.** Official subclass cards print Foundation,
   Specialization, or Mastery under the title. Homebrew ones print nothing, so a player holding three
   cards of the same subclass cannot tell them apart.
4. **Custom subclass tiers don't reliably link up.** Levelling up a subclass is supposed to hand the
   player the next card in the same family, but the link depends on an explicit family field most
   authors never fill in — they simply give all three cards the same name. When the link fails, the
   upgrade silently gives nothing, and there is no manual way to add the missing card either.
5. **An incomplete subclass fails silently.** An expansion can be saved and enabled with a Foundation
   and no Mastery. Nothing says so; the player finds out at level 10 when the upgrade does nothing.
6. **Most of the rulebook's loot is missing, and what's there looks like a sticky note.** Nineteen of
   the sixty loot items were never transcribed. Loot and consumables that *do* exist render through the
   generic card with a random flat color and a fallback plaque, so treasure reads as less real than a
   weapon. There is also no way to acquire any of it — the Loot tabs were removed from Add Gear.
7. **The Honing Relic does nothing.** It grants "+1 to an Experience of your choice", but the modifier
   engine has no concept of an Experience as a target, so the relic is inert text.
8. **Experiences are second-class.** They cannot be created from the New Card flow the way every other
   card can, and the +1 bonuses earned at level up are invisible in the Modifiers panel — the one place
   a player goes to answer "where is this number coming from?"

## Solution

1. The receive panel shows the card's **name only** — a clean title card, nothing to bleed. The card
   itself begins fully off-screen above the display and eases down into place over a longer, calmer
   arc, then tucks into the hand. The phone **vibrates the moment a tag is read**, before any UI paints,
   so the player knows the tap landed. And a shared card keeps its identity: images are inlined into
   the payload from every send path, and a weapon or armor card renders through the real equipment card
   — glyph, stat block, plaque and all — wherever it came from.
2. The level-up picker resolves the chosen card from the **carousel's live position at the moment of
   the press**, not from a lagging mirror of the index. The underlying latch that froze that mirror is
   removed, and the panel stops rebuilding the gesture mid-drag.
3. Custom subclass cards print a centered tier word under the title, matching the official scans.
4. The three tier cards **link by name, case-insensitively**, so authors get grouping for free by
   naming all three the same. The grouping is visible in the expansion's card list, and every tier is
   available in Add Gear as a manual fallback.
5. Enabling — or opening — an expansion whose subclass is missing a tier raises a warning that names
   the subclass and the missing tiers. It never blocks saving or enabling.
6. All 120 loot and consumable entries are present, and both families get their own forged card: a
   treasure-chest glyph on dug earth for loot, an alchemist's flask on apothecary glass for
   consumables, each with its own plaque family and the rulebook's table roll on a stat row. Loot and
   Consumable tabs return to Add Gear so they can be acquired.
7. Experiences become a first-class modifier target. Any card effect can point at any Experience the
   character has, chosen from a picker that lists them all; unset means the first one. The Honing Relic
   ships with that effect.
8. "Experience" joins the card-type picker, and choosing it in New Card creates a real Experience —
   targetable by item 7 and by level up. The Modifiers panel gains an Experiences section showing each
   Experience's base bonus, its level-up increases, and any relic effect, itemized by source.

## User Stories

1. As a player receiving a card, I want the confirmation to show just the card's name, so that nothing
   overflows the panel and I can read what I'm being offered at a glance.
2. As a player receiving a card, I want the card to descend from above the screen rather than appear,
   so that the arrival reads as an arrival.
3. As a player receiving a card, I want the descent to be unhurried, so that I can actually watch it.
4. As a player tapping two phones together, I want a vibration the instant the tag is read, so that I
   know it worked before anything appears on screen.
5. As a player sharing a card that has an uploaded image, I want the image to travel with it, so that
   the other player sees the card I made.
6. As a player sharing a weapon from my Card Library, I want it to arrive with its weapon decoration
   and stat block, so that it is the same card on both phones.
7. As a player sharing armor, I want the same fidelity as a weapon.
8. As a player who receives a weapon, I want it to look like a weapon everywhere it appears afterwards
   — in my hand, in the archive, in creation — not only at the moment I received it.
9. As a player levelling up, I want the domain card I centered to be the domain card I get, every time.
10. As a player levelling up, I want to press "Choose this card" immediately after scrolling without
    waiting, so that the picker never quietly disagrees with me.
11. As a player, I want the same guarantee anywhere that picker is used, so that creation and Add Gear
    can't develop the same fault.
12. As a homebrew author, I want my subclass cards to print Foundation / Specialization / Mastery under
    the title, so that they match the official cards.
13. As a homebrew author, I want to see that tier line in the editor's live preview, so that I can
    confirm it before saving.
14. As a homebrew author, I want to name all three of my subclass cards the same thing and have them
    link automatically, so that I don't have to learn a separate family field.
15. As a homebrew author, I want capitalization not to matter when linking, so that "Blood Mage" and
    "blood mage" are one subclass.
16. As a homebrew author, I want the explicit family field to still win when I do use it, so that I can
    give the three cards different titles if I want to.
17. As a homebrew author, I want subclass cards of different classes never to merge, even with the same
    name, so that two classes can both have a "Sentinel".
18. As a homebrew author, I want to see which cards are grouped as one subclass in the expansion's card
    list, so that I can verify the linking without levelling a character up.
19. As a player levelling up a homebrew subclass, I want the next tier card to be added automatically,
    so that the upgrade advancement actually does something.
20. As a player, I want every tier of a custom subclass to be available in Add Gear, so that I can add
    one by hand if I need to.
21. As a player enabling an expansion with an incomplete subclass, I want a warning naming the subclass
    and the missing tiers, so that I know before I build a character around it.
22. As a player opening such an expansion, I want the same warning, so that I see it even if it was
    already enabled.
23. As a homebrew author, I want the warning never to block saving or enabling, so that I can work on a
    pack in pieces.
24. As a player, I want all sixty rulebook loot items available, so that my GM can hand me any of them.
25. As a player, I want all sixty consumables available, for the same reason.
26. As a player, I want loot to look like loot — its own art, its own plaque — so that treasure feels
    like treasure and not like a note I typed.
27. As a player, I want consumables to look distinct from permanent loot, so that I can tell at a
    glance what I'll lose on use.
28. As a player, I want the item's table roll printed on the card, so that I can find it in the book.
29. As a player, I want a Loot tab in Add Gear, so that I can actually pick up what my GM gave me.
30. As a player, I want a Consumables tab, for the same reason.
31. As a player, I want to search or scan the loot list, so that finding one item among sixty isn't a
    chore.
32. As a player carrying a Stride Relic, I want my Agility to go up, so that the relic is real. (Already
    true; must stay true.)
33. As a player carrying the Honing Relic, I want +1 to one of my Experiences, so that the relic works.
34. As a player, I want to choose which Experience the Honing Relic boosts, so that it matches my
    character.
35. As a player, I want that choice to default to my first Experience, so that it does something
    sensible before I touch it.
36. As a player, I want the effects editor to list every Experience I have, however many that is, so
    that late-game characters aren't limited to their first two.
37. As a player, I want to change which Experience an effect targets later, so that I'm not locked in.
38. As a player, I want an effect pointing at an Experience I deleted to fail safely, so that the sheet
    never breaks.
39. As a player creating a card, I want "Experience" in the type picker, so that I can make one the same
    way I make everything else.
40. As a player, I want a card created as an Experience to become a real Experience, so that level up
    and the Honing Relic can both see it.
41. As a player, I want an Experience I create that way to start at the standard bonus, so that it
    matches one made at character creation.
42. As a player, I want to open the Modifiers panel and see each Experience with its current total, so
    that I know what I'm rolling with.
43. As a player, I want the level-up +1s itemized there by source, so that I can see where the number
    came from.
44. As a player, I want a relic's contribution itemized there too, so that the panel tells the whole
    story.
45. As a player with no Experiences, I want the panel to simply omit the section, so that it isn't
    cluttered.
46. As a player, I want all of the above to leave my existing characters working exactly as before, so
    that upgrading costs me nothing.

## Implementation Decisions

**Modules built or modified, and why.**

- **A subclass-family module (extracted, pure, tested).** Grouping logic lives beside the library card
  model as small pure functions: a family key that falls back from the explicit family field to the
  normalized title and is namespaced by class; a display name; and a detector that returns the families
  missing tiers with the missing tier words. Every consumer — level-up upgrade resolution, the gear
  browser, the expansion card list, the enable/open warning — routes through these, so the linking rule
  is defined exactly once. This is the deep module of this PRD: three functions, no UI, no I/O.
- **A shared library-card renderer (extracted).** Today each of the five places that draws an embedded
  homebrew card constructs the generic forged card inline, which is why weapons and armor lost their
  decoration in exactly five places at once. These collapse to one component that branches on content
  type: weapon and armor synthesize the equipment definitions the real equipment cards already take and
  delegate to them; subclass passes the tier word; everything else falls through to the generic card.
  Fixing fidelity once fixes it everywhere, and the NFC-received weapon is then correct for the rest of
  its life, not just during the receive ceremony.
- **The forged-card family gains a loot card and a loot glyph.** Modelled directly on the existing
  weapon and armor cards — same seam geometry, same footer, same stat-row primitive — with a new glyph
  pair and two new plaque themes. The plaque theme lookup is additive: the labels were already being
  passed and were falling through to the generic fallback.
- **The straight carousel gains a live centered-index accessor**, and its grind latch is always unwound
  on gesture finalize. The index published to parents is a *mirror*: it is deliberately suppressed while
  the gear strip is being ground, and it reaches the parent a React commit later. That is fine for
  labelling a button and wrong for committing a choice. The fix is therefore two-sided — remove the
  latch that could freeze the mirror indefinitely, and make the commit path stop trusting the mirror at
  all. The level-up panel additionally memoizes its item list, because an unstable identity there
  rebuilt the pan gesture on every detent and reconfigured a live native handler mid-drag, which is what
  produced the cancelled gestures in the first place. Creation already memoized; that is the differential
  that explains why only level up misbehaved.
- **The effect model gains an Experience target.** The target union is closed and backed by exhaustive
  records, so a new member has to be added in lockstep across the union, the base-stat literals, the
  label map, and the ordered target list. The instance is carried by a new optional experience-id field
  on the effect, because the target alone cannot name one of N objects. Unset resolves to the character's
  first Experience — that is what makes the Honing Relic work out of the box and what the picker offers
  by default.
- **Experience totals get their own breakdown function** rather than a row in the generic stat
  breakdown. The generic breakdown is a record keyed by target, which structurally cannot hold one value
  per Experience. A dedicated pure function returns, per Experience, the base bonus and the itemized
  contributions from level-up increases and from enabled cards. The Modifiers panel renders this as its
  own section. This also means the aggregate Experience entry in the generic breakdown is never
  displayed and carries no meaning.
- **The effects picker becomes a function of the character.** It is currently a module-level constant
  imported by its call sites; it grows an optional per-character Experiences group, which requires
  threading the character file to the two places the picker is opened. Matching an effect back to its
  picker option must compare the experience id as well, or switching between two Experiences silently
  keeps the first.
- **The card-type picker gains Experience, and the New Card save path routes on it.** Card types are
  otherwise purely cosmetic — they select a plaque theme and nothing else. This is the first type that
  changes where the card is stored, so the routing lives in the save handler, not in the type system.
- **The library screen loses nothing and gains warnings.** The existing modal-notice mechanism on that
  screen is reused rather than extracting the character sheet's transient toast, which is coupled to
  sheet types.

**Schema changes.** All additive and all optional; a file or expansion written by an earlier version
loads unchanged.

- Card effects gain an optional experience id.
- The effect target union gains an Experience member.
- No change to the library card shape — subclass family and tier fields already exist and were simply
  never displayed or defaulted from the title.

**Data.** The nineteen missing loot entries are transcribed from the rulebook's loot table and appended
in table-roll order. The loot data file was originally machine-generated by a script that does not live
in this repository; it becomes hand-maintained, and its header says so, so that a future regeneration
does not silently discard the additions.

**Interactions.**

- A card received by NFC that carries a catalog reference resolves to the real bundled card; otherwise
  it embeds. Unchanged, but now the embedded branch renders correctly.
- Enabling an expansion and opening an expansion both run the incomplete-subclass detector; the warning
  is advisory and the action proceeds either way.
- An effect targeting a deleted Experience contributes nothing rather than throwing.

## Testing Decisions

A good test here asserts **externally observable behavior** — what a function returns for a given
character or card set — and never reaches into how it got there. The extracted pure modules exist
largely so that this PRD's risky logic is testable without a device.

Tested:

- **Subclass family grouping.** Same title different case links; explicit family field wins over title;
  different classes with the same name stay separate; the incomplete detector reports exactly the
  missing tier words and reports nothing for a complete family and nothing for a card set with no
  subclasses.
- **Experience modifiers.** An effect with no experience id resolves to the first Experience; an effect
  naming a specific Experience contributes only to that one; an effect naming a deleted Experience
  contributes nothing; level-up increases and card effects both appear as itemized contributions; a
  character with no Experiences produces an empty result.
- **Loot data integrity.** Ids unique across the whole loot namespace and round-tripping through the
  accessor (existing invariant, now covering the new entries); both tables complete at sixty entries
  each with contiguous rolls; every declared effect names a real target; loot ids do not collide with
  weapon, armor, or catalog ids — the effect resolver checks loot last, so a collision would shadow
  silently.

Not tested automatically: the receive ceremony's motion, the descent timing, the haptic, and the carousel
gesture behavior. Per this project's standing convention, motion and loaders are verified on device by
the owner rather than screenshot-tested. The carousel fix is structural — the accessor either reads the
live position or it does not — and the assertion that matters is a human one.

Prior art in this codebase: the data-integrity suite for the loot invariants, the card-effects suite for
the modifier resolution cases, and the NFC gate suite for the shape of a small pure predicate with an
exhaustive per-input test.

## Out of Scope

- Reinstating a Loot tier filter. Loot has no tier; the tabs filter by family only.
- Art for loot items. They are code-forged, like weapons and armor; there are no loot scans.
- Making Experiences their own carousel category. They continue to ride the arsenal hand.
- Gating level-up domain options by enabled expansion. This was noticed during investigation and is a
  genuine separate gap, but adding a filter *removes* cards from a list players currently see, which is
  a behavior change that deserves its own decision.
- Extracting the character sheet's transient toast into a shared component for the library warnings.
- Expansion provenance for a single card shared over NFC. A lone card still arrives as a standalone
  embedded card with a fresh id and does not update-merge into its parent expansion.

## Further Notes

The NFC weapon-fidelity problem was misdiagnosed by its symptom. Nothing about the NFC transport drops
weapon decoration — the payload carries the full mechanical spec faithfully. The card renders flat
because embedded homebrew cards and catalog equipment are two disjoint render paths, and the embedded
one has always used the generic card. The same flatness is therefore visible for a homebrew weapon that
was never shared at all. Fixing the renderer rather than the transport fixes both.

The level-up bug's two contributing faults are worth keeping distinct in review. The latched grind value
is the mechanism that made the mirror stale; the unmemoized item list is the trigger that produced the
cancelled gestures. Either fix alone makes the bug rarer. Reading the live position at commit time makes
it structurally impossible, which is why all three are being done.

Loot was found to be roughly two-thirds present rather than absent — the consumables table was complete
and the loot table began at roll twenty. The transcription work was consequently much smaller than the
"120 items" framing implies, and the real gap was that none of it was reachable or visually distinct.
