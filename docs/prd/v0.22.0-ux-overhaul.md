# v0.22.0 — UX overhaul: character state, DM rework, onboarding, authoring, and the language/text/resilience passes

Source: `docs/ux-audit-v0.21.0.md` (scored 15/40 on Nielsen's heuristics) plus the owner's per-item
decisions. Tablet findings are deferred to `docs/tablet-audit-v0.21.0.md` and are **out of scope here**.

## Problem Statement

RuneKeep is made far better than it is explained. The character sheet's interaction design, the
loading states, the hold-to-confirm pattern and the NFC receive ceremony are all excellent, but the
app teaches none of it, contradicts itself between screens, and cannot undo anything.

Concretely, as a player:

- I can lose ten steps of character creation to a mis-tap, with no warning and no draft saved.
- I can permanently level up or apply a rest on a single tap, in an app where deleting one card
  demands a 620 ms hold.
- I can delete a card and never get it back.
- I have no way to see what my character looked like before tonight's session, or to walk back a
  mistake made three encounters ago.
- I cannot open a `.rkp` a friend sent me on WhatsApp; I have to find it in a file picker.
- Long names, long modifier explanations and three-digit HP silently clip or overlap, because most
  text on the sheet lives in a hard-clipping fixed box.
- One concept has four different names depending on which screen I am on, and error messages show me
  developer diagnostics like "Card 3 missing id".

And as a DM, the half of the product I use is visibly weaker than the half I don't: 21 font sizes
where the menu uses 3, 25 arbitrary spacing values, no focal point on any screen, a primary action
smaller than the decoration beside it, and a "desaturated" palette contradicted by the gold border,
gold dialogs, gold keypad and gold loading screen that appear on every single DM surface.

## Solution

Nine coordinated changes, shipped as a sequence of merged PRs:

1. **Character State** — a new float-menu interface replacing Modifiers, holding the existing
   modifier breakdown (with its wrapping fixed) plus a complete, rewindable timeline of everything
   that has ever happened to the character. No mutation is exempt.
2. **A card trash** so deletion is recoverable without a general undo stack.
3. **Creation safety** — the draft survives, and leaving asks first.
4. **A full DM UI overhaul**, not the five patches the audit proposed.
5. **Optional onboarding** that actually teaches the gesture vocabulary, skippable and re-openable.
6. **Card authoring rework** — a genuinely quick path and a complete path, plus a better gear browser.
7. **`.rkp` file association**, so the format opens from WhatsApp or any file manager, with an import
   route that is safe from whatever screen the user is on.
8. **Language, text-fit and resilience passes** across the whole app.
9. **The elf rest rule**, correct under mixed ancestry.

The gesture model (taps and long presses) is explicitly **not** changing; the owner is happy with it.

### Character State and history

12. As a player, I want the confirmation to tell me exactly what I am about to lose, including how

13. As a player, I want to be warned that once I change anything after rewinding, the discarded

14. As a player, I want to be able to browse the timeline and back out without changing anything, so

    was restored.
19. As a DM, I do not want a player's rewind to silently rewrite the party vitals I am tracking, so

## Implementation Decisions

### Character history: full snapshots, not an event log

Chosen over replay after inventorying all mutation sites. Three findings made replay untenable:

- **Deterministic id collision.** Level-up mints Experience ids from array length. Rewind past a
  level-up, author a card (which appends to the same array), redo the level-up, and it mints an id
  that already exists. Snapshots never replay, so this cannot occur.
- **Irreversible side effects.** NFC broadcast, `.rkp` written to the share sheet, forged PNGs,
  files deleted from disk. A replay would re-run these. A snapshot restore does not.
- **Loading is not pure.** Parsing back-fills subclass cards into the character, so a snapshot
  written and re-read is not byte-identical to what was written. Any replay-equality check would
  produce false positives.

Structural sharing keeps snapshots cheap: unchanged sub-objects are retained by reference, so a
resource change does not duplicate the card arrays.

### The one choke point

In-play mutations funnel through a single save closure in the sheet, with creation and import as the
only other write entry points. History is recorded at those three places and nowhere else. This is
what makes "no action exempt" tractable rather than a 40-site audit.

Two hazards at that choke point must be handled explicitly:

- The save closure unconditionally stamps live resources and gold onto **every** write, so a purely
  structural change still carries the player's HP at that instant. History labelling must classify an
  entry by its structural diff, ignoring resource fields, or every equip will be mislabelled as an HP
  change.
- Two mutation paths call the save closure from inside a state updater, which double-invokes under
  React StrictMode. The history hook must be idempotent per commit, keyed on identity, not appended
  blindly.

### Coalescing

Three tiers, all in the pure module:

- **Same-field rapid edits** merge within a time window into one entry carrying the net delta and the
  individual steps. Mirrors the debounce that already governs resource persistence.
- **Bulk equip** issues one save per card, milliseconds apart. These collapse into a single
  "Equipped 8 cards" entry by intent, not by timing.
- **Milestones** (creation, level up, rest, death) never coalesce and are pinned in the UI.

### Rewind semantics

Linear history with truncation. Rewinding to entry *n* restores that snapshot and marks the timeline
rewound; the entries after *n* remain visible and greyed until the next mutation, at which point they
are discarded permanently and the timeline continues from *n*. This is the owner's stated rule: once
you change something, you can never fast-forward again. Browsing is free; only a hold-to-confirm
commits a rewind.

**Restored state is validated, not trusted.** A raw snapshot bypasses the mutation layer's guards
(never delete the last card; gold, companion, beastform and tracker cards are undeletable; at least
one category enabled). Rewind runs the restored file through a repair pass and reports anything it
had to correct. Resources are re-clamped against the restored maxima, which is lossy by design and
must be surfaced rather than hidden.

**Rewind is character-scoped.** It does not touch party global vitals, folder assignments, the card
library, DM encounters, or files on disk. Where the restored state disagrees with an external store,
the confirmation says so plainly.

### Storage and size

Character files reference catalog art by id but inline player-authored card bodies, and NFC-received
cards embed base64 images that can reach tens of kilobytes. Snapshots would be dominated by immutable
image payloads, so image data is content-addressed and stored once outside the snapshot chain, with
snapshots holding references.

History is capped with milestones preferentially retained, and is **stripped on export**. Characters
are not shared over NFC (the app already refuses hero tags and directs the user to file import), so
the NFC size ceiling does not apply here; stripping is about keeping shared files small and not
handing someone else a play log.

### Schema migration is a prerequisite

The parser hard-rejects any file whose version is not exactly 1, with no migration hook. Adding
history bumps the schema, and without a migration path every historical snapshot becomes unloadable
the moment the version changes again. A migration function lands **before** history, not after.

### The State interface

Replaces Modifiers in the float menu's north slot. Two views: the existing modifier breakdown,
unchanged except that contributor and stat labels wrap instead of truncating; and the timeline.

### Rest and the elf rule

The move limit becomes a pure function of the character rather than the constant 2. Elf's rest bonus
rides its **second** feature, so under mixed ancestry it survives when elf is the second pick and is
lost when elf is the first pick, matching the existing cross-out rule where the first pick keeps
feature 1 and the second keeps feature 2. The limit is a maximum, not a requirement.

The existing ancestry-effect map covers only ancestries granting permanent stat passives, so the rest
bonus needs its own mapping rather than being folded into that one.

### `.rkp` routing

An Android intent filter claims the extension across content, file and mail schemes. Because the
format has no registered MIME type, matching is by path pattern and the payload is sniffed after
opening rather than trusted from the intent.

Routing is a pure decision function: given the file's parsed kind, whether it collides with an
existing character, and what the user is currently doing, it returns one of import here, ask first,
warn about overwrite, or defer. Deferral parks the payload so the user can finish what they are doing
and act on it from the menu. Nothing imports without an explicit confirmation.

### DM overhaul

A DM type scale of five real steps replaces 21 ad-hoc sizes; a three-value spacing rule replaces 25;
auto-shrink stops being the default for row titles. The four shared components with no DM variant
gain one, and the screen border gets a desaturated twin, which is what makes the mode read as
coherent. Rounded corners in the adversary library return to chamfers without giving back the scroll
performance the rewrite bought. Every empty state adopts the poster composition the parties screen
already uses.

### Text fit

Shared helpers for the recurring cases, so fixes cannot silently regress: label truncation with a
legible floor, wrap-aware panel titles, and a font-scaling cap applied to the fixed-box primitives on
the sheet while leaving flex-laid-out screens free to scale.

## Testing Decisions

Good tests here assert **external behaviour**: given a character and a sequence of user-level
actions, what is persisted, what the timeline says, and what a rewind produces. They must not assert
internal shapes that will churn. Prior art is the existing colocated pure-module suite in `src/lib`,
which already covers levelling, rest, modifiers, damage, companion, favorites, folders, party,
session and the file format.

Four modules get coverage, per the owner:

**Character history engine.** Recording every mutation kind; coalescing same-field edits inside and
outside the window; bulk equip collapsing to one entry; milestones never coalescing; rewind restoring
exactly; truncate-on-next-change and the guarantee that fast-forward becomes impossible; retention
capping while preserving milestones; repair of an invalid restored state; resource re-clamping;
idempotency under a doubled commit.

**Rest engine including the elf rule.** Extends the existing suite: two moves by default; three for a
pure elf; three when elf is the second mixed pick; two when elf is the first pick; fewer than the
maximum always allowed; the limit reflected in the panel's own wording.

**`.rkp` import routing.** The decision function across valid character, valid expansion, corrupt
payload, wrong kind, id collision with an existing character, and arrival while the user is mid-edit
elsewhere. Error messages assert the user-facing text, not the thrown string.

**Text-fit and language helpers.** Truncation floors, wrap behaviour, and the canonical label lookup,
so the language pass cannot drift back.

## Out of Scope

- **Everything in `docs/tablet-audit-v0.21.0.md`.** Deferred by the owner until this lands.
- **Changing the gesture model.** Taps and long presses stay exactly as they are.
- **A general undo stack.** The card trash plus history rewind covers the need; a live undo does not.
- **Class-change wiping subclass and domain picks.** Owner considers this expected behaviour.
- **Search.** The audit flagged the absence of search everywhere but the adversary library; it is not
  in this release.
- **Renaming a character after creation.** Currently impossible by design; unchanged here.
- **Card art resolution.** Source-limited, per the audit.
- **iOS file association.** Android is the shipping target.

## Further Notes

The audit's full evidence, including per-file citations, lives in `docs/ux-audit-v0.21.0.md`. The
mutation inventory that drove the history architecture is summarised in the Implementation Decisions
above; the significant risks it surfaced and how each is handled are listed there rather than left
implicit, because they are the reason the design is snapshot-based.

Two items are worth confirming against real data during implementation: whether Elf's Celestial
Trance is genuinely the second feature on the printed card (the rulebook ordering says yes, and the
implementation follows the same first-keeps-one, second-keeps-two rule as every other mixed
ancestry), and whether any live character triggers the parser's subclass back-fill, which is the one
place loading mutates what it loaded.

A **second resilience pass** is scheduled after history lands, at the owner's request, because
introducing state history changes several of the failure modes catalogued in the audit.
